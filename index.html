About this map

Routes are reconstructed from the book of Acts using traditional identifications of ancient place names. Lines show approximate sea and land connections between stops, not literal roads or shipping lanes, and distances are straight-line (great-circle) estimates — actual travel would have followed coastlines, winds, and roads, and taken longer.

Ancient region names reflect Roman provinces around the time of Paul's journeys, with one exception: Arabia was still the independent Nabataean Kingdom in Paul's day, not yet annexed as the Roman province of Arabia Petraea (that happened in AD 106). Modern country names are simplified and approximate, shown for geographic orientation only — they don't reflect a position on any disputed border. One historical note: ancient Antioch (modern Antakya) is today part of Turkey, not Syria, following a 1939 border change.

Letter attributions follow traditional, widely-held datings; a few (noted inline) are genuinely disputed among scholars. The Pastoral Epistles (1–2 Timothy, Titus) aren't shown — tradition places them after a release and further travel that Acts doesn't narrate, so they fall outside this map.

Gold stars mark churches Paul founded, strengthened, or wrote to. Blue diamonds mark places with especially significant events in the biblical narrative. Tap either symbol for its story and Scripture references.
